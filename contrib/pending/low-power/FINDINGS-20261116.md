# Low power on the J716s: findings of the night of 2026-11-15/16

Branch `lowpower` (on `j716s-next2`), boots low-power-1..3; integrated into `j716s-next3`.

## What works

- **s2idle with an automatic wake.** The watchdog's WD0 bark interrupt (IRQ 721, ADT) wakes s2idle after
  `apple_wdt.s2idle_wake_s` seconds; WD1 resets the Mac `s2idle_fallback_s` (60) seconds later if the wake never comes.
  low-power-2: 30 s and 120 s sleeps woke by the timer. low-power-3: five sleeps, `suspend_stats` success=7 fail=0.
  next3-1: same, with device health identical before and after.
- The RTC alarm cannot wake s2idle here: the PMU alarm sits behind the generation-4 SPMI controller, whose interrupts the
  driver refuses, and `rtc-macsmc` has no alarm. Lid, power button and keyboard can wake it.
- After the wake: panel, Wi-Fi (ping), Bluetooth, both sound cards, USB3 devices, both Type-C partners and the USB4 PCIe
  tunnel (Apollo) come back. The tunnel's `resume_noirq` -110 of low-power-2 is fixed by keeping its link through s2idle
  (`ab1837c6a812`).

## Power

- The battery gauge's `charge_now`/`energy_now` (B0RM) are a remaining-capacity estimate that moves by tens of mAh
  between reads: not usable over minutes. `power_now` (B0AP) is an averaged power.
- s2idle drain: at most about 4.4 W (B0AP right after each wake: -4.50, -4.40, -4.29 W; awake on battery -10 to -15 W).
  low-power-4 measured it at 4.3-4.4 W (below).
- cpuidle's s2idle residency reads ~0 because the last CPU into s2idle stops sched_clock (`tick_freeze()`); only entries
  count (about 6 per CPU per 150 s: DCP, SMC, AOP mailbox and I2C interrupts, all `IRQF_NO_SUSPEND`).
- What stays powered in s2idle (from code): the DCP with its display domains (no full shutdown; `appledrm.power_gate_ms`
  works only on the M4 display firmware), the GPU firmware (domains always-on for the loader hand-off; cores power off
  when idle), the Thunderbolt link with a PCIe tunnel (teardown skips tunnelled ports; ~1.3 W on t6000 per the driver),
  the N1 PCIe link, NVMe (only a firmware sleep shuts it down). Panel and backlight are off; clusters at 300 MHz; memory
  and fabric floors 0.
- Awake idle: windows alternate ~10.8 W and 15-16 W (compositor redraws: up to 450k function-call IPIs per minute plus DCP
  and GPU mailbox traffic). Whenever the GPU reports work the CLPC holds memory/fabric at vote 0x26. Idle comparisons need
  a static screen.

## CPU frequency and idle

- `idle=nop arm64.nowfxt` come from the loader (`kboot.c`), harmless on current upstream (T6050 idles in WFI anyway; WFxT
  treated as absent).
- No deeper CPU idle state than WFI exists without a PSCI monitor or PMP-driven cluster power-down on M4+.
- Lazy verification on T6050 lets an idle cluster drop to 300 MHz when nothing periodic runs on it (low-power-1:
  policy0 at 300 MHz 55 of 60 s; next2 kept the M clusters mostly at 1344 MHz). From low-power-2 on, M cluster 0 and the
  P cluster spend half of every idle minute at their top frequency instead: see low-power-5 below (an RT audio loop).
- CLPC is built in; the GPU's `apple,clpc` link is restored (GPU work raises memory/fabric floors); `clpc.gpu_floor` switch.

## USB3 left-front "host [device]"

Two lowpower boots came up with the left-front hub USB2-only and the port in the device data role; usb-aon was ruled out
(the thunderbolt kernel with the same commit had SuperSpeed). low-power-3 and next3-1 had SuperSpeed. Best lead: a Type-C
role race at boot (these kernels reach xHCI 0.7-2 s earlier). Not reproduced since.

## low-power-4 (s2p-20261116-053208): the s2idle number, B0AP's window, parking

- **B0AP's window is short.** After force-discharge (awake, ~10.1 W) power_now read 0, -7.20 W at 1 s, -10.10 W at
  2 s and stayed there: the SMC updates it about once a second over a window of 1-2 s. PSTR (system total power)
  behaves the same, updating every 1-2 s.
- **s2idle power: about 4.3-4.4 W** (three sleeps, cables as before: Anker USB3 hub, Thunderbolt Apollo, charger,
  debug cable). The first read after each wake still holds the SMC's last sample, taken at the end of the sleep:
  B0AP -4.29 / -4.34 / -4.38 W and PSTR 4.40 / 4.46 / 4.49 W, two independent sensors agreeing (low-power-3: -4.50,
  -4.40, -4.29 W). The next update already mixes in the resume (-7.69, -6.85, -4.77 W), then awake ~9 W. At most a
  fraction of the resume is in that first sample, so the sleep is at most ~4.4 W and probably not below ~4 W.
- **Radios: no measurable share in s2idle.** Wi-Fi + BT blocked: 4.34 W / PSTR 4.46 W, inside the two defaults'
  4.29-4.38 / 4.40-4.49 W.
- **Panel backlight at half brightness: 0.88 W awake** (PSTR 10.85 W vs 9.97 W at brightness 0).
- So, of ~9-10 W awake idle on the battery, s2idle removes ~5 W; the remaining ~4.4 W is what stays powered (list above):
  DCP and display domains, the Thunderbolt link with its tunnel, the hub's link, GPU firmware, PD controllers, fabric
  and DRAM at their idle states, and the always-on SoC. The next candidates to measure are the Thunderbolt link (unplug
  the Apollo, or ACIO teardown once it handles PCIe tunnels) and the DCP (a power-down for firmware 27).
- **Parking never ran at all: a bug, fixed in `3ab74fdab59c`.** Every counter, parks and every skip reason, stayed 0 on
  all three policies for 14 minutes, so the idle hook never found a cluster for any CPU. `->init()` took the cluster's
  CPUs from `policy->related_cpus`, which the cpufreq core fills only after `->init()` returns: the park mask stayed
  empty and no CPU got its per-CPU cluster pointer. The bug is in the upstream branch commit (`6b72af7a8c93`,
  `pwr/apple-cluster-idle-park`), so it affects every SoC that commit targets. Fixed by taking `policy->cpus`. Built, not booted: parking has never run on a T6050. It writes the DVFS
  register from the idle path, so the first boot with it live must check for "stopping requests" / DVFS timeouts. `j716s-next3` carries the inert version.
  low-power-5 booted it (below).

## low-power-5 (test-20261116-061803): parking live, and what keeps two clusters at their top state

- **Parking runs without errors.** With `3ab74fdab59c`: no "stopping requests", no DVFS timeout, load still reaches the
  top states and falls back, all seven s2idle cycles rc=0 and timer-woken. Clusters also park on the way into s2idle
  (the WFI-park state is the s2idle state).
- **It parks only M cluster 1 (policy6):** 5263 / 5999 / 3656 / 1111 parks and 12.5 / 9.9 / 5.0 / 1.9 s parked in the
  four park windows. M cluster 0 (policy0) and the P cluster (policy12) parked ~1400 / ~700 times at boot and almost
  never afterwards (a few around each s2idle).
- **Why those two never park: most likely a periodic real-time thread, not CPU0's interrupts** (inferred; the skip
  counters of that boot were not saved). In every idle
  window of low-power-2, -3 and -5, with parking on or off, policy0 makes ~10,000 transitions a minute and spends
  ~30 s at 4380 MHz and ~30 s at 1344 MHz (never 300). From low-power-3 on, policy12 does the same between 300 and
  4608 MHz. low-power-1 had neither (policy0 at 300 MHz 55 s, ~400 transitions). `top` in the same windows shows
  `pipewire` at ~24 % CPU on an idle desktop. Its data loop runs as a real-time thread (rtkit), here apparently ~85 wakes a second
  (two transitions per wake; presumably the j716 convolver graph running while silent). RT tasks get `uclamp_min` 1024 by default, so schedutil asks for the top
  state at each wake. The cluster wakes again within a few ms, so the menu governor probably picks the shallow WFI state (not
  the 1 ms WFI-park state) on that CPU, and the cluster is never fully parked (skip reason "awake"). Exactly the case
  the upstream parking commit names, but here the idle gaps are too short for its 1 ms target residency.
  - Confirm on any running boot, without a new kernel: `grep . /sys/devices/system/cpu/cpufreq/policy*/park_stats`
    (expect `awake` to dominate on policy0/12), `ps -eLo pid,tid,cls,rtprio,psr,pcpu,comm | grep -i pipewire`.
  - Cost: two clusters half the time at their top voltage. This is likely the largest idle item left besides the panel.
  - Candidate fixes, in order of safety:
    1. Make pipewire suspend the graph when silent (session-manager `session.suspend-timeout-seconds` / node
       `suspend` for the convolver chain), so nothing runs at idle.
    2. Lower `kernel.sched_util_clamp_min_rt_default` (e.g. 256): RT audio still gets a floor, not the top state.
       Watch for xruns.
    3. Give parking a state the menu governor picks for short gaps (target residency ~200 us), measured.
- **Power:** park-1 10.885 W vs nopark-1 11.035 W (quiet pair); nopark-2 15.03 W vs park-2 16.00 W (busy pair). The
  difference (-0.15 W quiet, +1 W busy) is inside the pairs' own spread. No measurable gain.
- **Keep parking?** Yes, with the fix: it is correct (no DVFS errors in a full run with seven sleeps), it parks the one
  cluster that idles long enough, it parks every cluster for s2idle, and `idle_park=0` turns it off. Do not expect
  power from it until the RT audio loop stops running at idle.
- The `write rc=1` came from the Type-C probe at the end of the load phase. Port 1 (right, the Apollo in Thunderbolt
  mode) read `[device]`, and the typec class refused the data-role write (EPERM). The left-front port was `[host]` with
  SuperSpeed, so the USB3/role race did not reproduce (third boot in a row).

## next4 (2026-11-16 morning): the top-state cause, traced (supersedes the PipeWire reading above)

- **It is speakerguardd v2's RT threads, not PipeWire.** `sched_switch` + `cpu_frequency` over 2 s on next4: CPU0 and
  CPU12 alternate only between idle and `sgv2-helper` / `sgv2-pcm` (SCHED_FIFO 10, ~1970 switches each in 2 s), and
  policy0's frequency goes 4380 MHz at each wake and 1344 MHz ~6 ms later, every 12.2 ms (~82 Hz). Hence exactly 50 % of
  every minute at the top state on policy0 and policy12, whatever else runs. The kernel has no `CONFIG_UCLAMP_TASK`, so
  schedutil gives any runnable RT task the top state (`sched_util_clamp_min_rt_default` does not exist).
- **PipeWire is a separate, smaller cost:** its data loop is a normal (TS) thread at 12-25 % of one core, running the
  sgv2 protect stage on the dummy driver (quantum 1024, 47 Hz) by design (speakerguardd-session: "the protect stage keeps
  running on the dummy driver (its heartbeat and thermal model never stop)"). Forcing quantum 4096 for a minute cut
  its CPU ~60 % and changed neither the top-state share nor the transitions.
- **Omarchy's screensaver** (hypridle-independent here: alacritty `org.omarchy.screensaver` + `ttfx --frame-rate 120`)
  was running in some idle windows: ~7700 function-call IPIs/s (tty writes and wakeups across CPUs), DCP mailbox
  560/s vs 24/s without it. Idle measurements must make sure it is not on. hypridle was not running at all on next4,
  so the 10-minute lock never fires either.
- **Fix built, not booted:** `CONFIG_UCLAMP_TASK=y` in `j716s.config` (`dbe7372ad0af`, `kbuild-next4b`); then
  `sysctl kernel.sched_util_clamp_min_rt_default=<N>` (try 0 and 256) and compare policy0/12 top-state share, park
  counts and B0AP on battery. Upstream question for aurora-silicon/speakerguardd: why the helper and pcm threads tick at
  82 Hz while `play=0` (amps unlinked, PCM closed).

## Realistic target without deep sleep

Aurora reports 1.35-2.65 W s2idle on J616s/J413-class machines. Here, with the DCP running, a trained Thunderbolt link, a
USB3 hub and the debug cable, s2idle measures 4.3-4.4 W; 2-3 W expected without cables and with radios quiet. Lower needs
deep sleep (see `../deep-sleep/NOTES.md`) or DCP power-down on firmware 27 plus tunnel-aware Thunderbolt teardown.
